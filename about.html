Extended Bio

From Accra to Stanford,
building with purpose.

I am a sophomore studying Computer Science (AI track) at Stanford University, with additional study for a minor in Earth Systems. I came to Stanford as an international student from Accra, Ghana, on a full ride for a $400,000 Leland Scholarship covering meals, tuition, room, and board.

My long-term goal is to launch a climate tech startup at the intersection of AI, machine learning, and human-computer interaction, while building an accelerator for African startups. The work I am most proud of so far sits at the meeting point of software and social impact: a published iOS app shipped to thousands of users, a Flutter safety app that won a national hackathon, and earlier work upcycling plastic waste into school furniture in Ghana.

I think of education as liberation and self-discovery. My journey from a limited set of high school options in Ghana, through PRESEC's General Arts track, to Stanford's open curriculum mirrors what Plato describes in the Allegory of the Cave. That framing shapes how I talk about my own path and how I think about the kind of access I want my future work to create for others.

When I'm not coding, you'll find me deeply embedded in Football Twitter culture (which became the subject of my PWR 2 paper), learning to swim at Stanford, or exploring Japanese, American, and Indian/Pakistani cuisine around the Bay Area. My partner is based at Kenyatta University in Nairobi, and I split my time between Ujamaa House and Accra during breaks.

My Philosophy

What drives me.

01
Education as Liberation
I believe access to quality education is the most powerful lever for changing lives and communities. The Leland Scholarship made Stanford possible for me — I want to build similar pathways for others.
02
AI for Climate & Africa
Technology should serve the places and people who need it most — not just the wealthiest markets. My AI/ML building focus on adapting AI to smallholder agriculture and climate resilience.
03
Building in Public
I share what I learn so others can follow. Open source, open notes, open doors. The projects I build are documented, the work I do is shared, and the accelerator I'm building is designed to be replicated.
